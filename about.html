The premise

Becoming mayor is not a campaign. It is a decade.

Most coaching and consulting in American politics is built for the year before the election. We are built for the ten years before that.

The mayor of a city is the most visible, most accountable, and most personally consequential elected position most Americans will ever hold. The job demands fluency in things that take years to learn: the budget, the comprehensive plan, the zoning code, the labor agreements, the unwritten understandings between departments. None of that can be cammed in the final six months.

So we work backwards. We help members identify the city they intend to serve, the public posture they intend to hold, and the issues they intend to know better than anyone else in the room. Then we help them show up, week after week, year after year, until the city already knows their name when the campaign begins.

The work is mostly unglamorous. That is the point.

A member's week might include reading a 200-page budget document, attending one routine commission meeting, writing one short post about what they learned, and having one coffee with a neighborhood leader. None of those activities are interesting on their own. Done for ten years, they become a track record.

We meet quarterly to review what was done, course-correct what was missed, and decide what comes next. The community is small by design. We accept new members by application only — read by a Review Panel, decided on vision and character.

The work

Small acts, repeated.
