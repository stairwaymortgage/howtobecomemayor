Requirements to Run for Mayor

Jim BlackburnMarch 20268 min read

The requirements to run for mayor in the United States are far simpler than most people expect — and understanding them is the first legal step on the path to becoming mayor of your city.

The requirements to run for mayor in the United States are far simpler than most people expect. In the vast majority of American cities, you need to meet four basic criteria: be a resident of the city, be a registered voter, meet a minimum age requirement, and either pay a filing fee or collect a required number of petition signatures. That is it. No college degree. No political party affiliation. No prior elected experience. No minimum income or net worth.

What makes this important is that the legal requirements to run for mayor create almost no barrier to entry. The real barriers — the ones that actually determine whether a candidate wins or loses — are credibility, trust, and community relationships built over years. Understanding the requirements to run for mayor takes about thirty minutes of research. Building the foundation to win takes a decade of consistent civic engagement.

According to the U.S. Election Assistance Commission, voter registration and candidacy requirements vary by state and municipality — which means the specific requirements to run for mayor in your city require a quick check of your city clerk's website or your state's division of elections. The framework below applies broadly across most American cities.

The Four Core Requirements to Run for Mayor

Residency. You must live in the city you are running to lead. Most cities require that you have been a resident for a specified period before the election — typically ranging from 30 days to one year depending on the jurisdiction. Some cities require residency within a specific district for commission races but allow citywide residency for the mayor's seat. Verify your specific city's requirement with the city clerk.

Voter registration. You must be a registered voter in the city. In most states, voter registration is a straightforward online or paper process. If you are not yet registered in your city, do that first. It is the foundational legal step that must be completed before any other candidacy paperwork is filed.

Age minimum. Most cities require mayoral candidates to be at least 18 years old — the same minimum as voter registration. A small number of cities have higher age requirements of 21 or 25, but these are uncommon. There is no maximum age to run for mayor in any American city.

Filing fee or petition signatures. To get your name on the ballot you must either pay a filing fee or collect a required number of valid petition signatures from registered voters in your city. Filing fees for mayor races in mid-size cities typically range from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars. Petition signature requirements typically range from 500 to 2,000 signatures depending on city size. Some cities offer both options. How to Find the Specific Requirements for Your City

The requirements to run for mayor vary enough between cities that you should verify the specifics for your municipality rather than relying on general guidance. Here is exactly how to find them.

Start with your city clerk's website. Search for "candidate filing" or "run for office" or "election information." The city clerk is the official custodian of election processes and almost always publishes candidate filing requirements, deadlines, and fees on their website. If you cannot find the information online, call the city clerk's office directly — they are required to provide this information to any resident who asks.

Your state's division of elections website is the second resource. Most states maintain centralized election portals that include links to local municipality requirements. Searching your state name plus "division of elections" or "secretary of state elections" will typically surface this resource. USA.gov maintains a directory of state and local government election resources that can help you navigate to the right starting point for your state.

Ballotpedia also maintains city-by-city election information that includes filing deadlines, candidate requirements, and recent election results for thousands of American cities. It is an excellent secondary source for confirming what you find on official government sites. Understanding how local elections work in detail helps you move through the filing process with confidence.

Campaign Finance Requirements — What Most Candidates Miss

Beyond the candidate filing requirements, there is a parallel set of campaign finance requirements that apply the moment you begin raising or spending money for a mayoral campaign. These requirements are separate from the candidate filing process and are often the area where first-time candidates make costly mistakes.

In most states, you must designate a campaign treasurer before accepting any campaign contributions or making any campaign expenditures. You must open a dedicated campaign bank account — personal and campaign funds cannot be legally commingled. You must file periodic financial disclosure reports showing all contributions received and expenditures made. Failure to comply with campaign finance requirements can result in fines, disqualification from the ballot, or in serious cases, criminal charges.

The requirements to run for mayor include campaign finance compliance from day one — not just after you formally announce. If a neighbor hands you a check at a neighborhood meeting and says it is for your future campaign, that is a campaign contribution that requires proper reporting. Consult your city clerk and ideally a local election attorney before accepting any money for a campaign. Understanding how much it costs to run for mayor and how to manage campaign finances legally is essential preparation for any serious candidate.

Party Affiliation — Is It Required

No party affiliation is required to run for mayor in the vast majority of American cities. According to the National League of Cities, approximately 75 percent of American city elections are nonpartisan — meaning candidates do not run on party labels and party affiliation does not appear on the ballot. This is one of the most significant differences between local elections and state or national elections.

In nonpartisan cities, party connections can still matter informally — organized party volunteers may work for candidates they favor, and party donor networks can be a source of campaign contributions. But the formal requirement of party nomination that exists in many state and national races simply does not apply in most local elections.

For an outsider candidate running without the support of an established political party, this is a structural advantage. You are not required to win a primary, secure a party endorsement, or appeal to a party base. You need to win the trust of the general electorate — which is exactly what the 14-year arc of civic engagement is designed to do. Running for mayor without a political party is not just possible in most American cities — it is the norm.

People Also Ask
What is the minimum age to run for mayor in the United States?
Most American cities require candidates to be at least 18 years old — the same minimum as voter registration. A small number of cities require 21 or 25, but these are uncommon. There is no maximum age requirement to run for mayor in any American city.
Do I need to be a registered voter to run for mayor?
Yes. Voter registration in the city you wish to represent is a universal requirement to run for mayor across virtually all American municipalities. It is the foundational legal step — complete it before any other candidacy preparation. The first step to becoming mayor includes verifying your voter registration is current and active in your city.
How long do I need to live in a city before I can run for mayor?
Residency requirements vary by city — typically from 30 days to one year before the election. Verify your specific city's requirement with the city clerk. The longer you have lived in your city, the stronger your credibility as a candidate regardless of the legal minimum.
What paperwork do I need to file to get on the mayoral ballot?
The candidate filing packet from your city clerk typically includes a declaration of candidacy, a financial disclosure form, and either a filing fee payment or a completed petition with the required number of valid voter signatures. Here is the complete guide to getting your name on the ballot.
Do I need party affiliation to run for mayor?
No — approximately 75 percent of American city elections are nonpartisan, meaning no party label appears on the ballot and no party nomination is required. Running for mayor without a political party is the norm in most American cities, not the exception.
Are there criminal record restrictions for running for mayor?
In most states, a criminal record does not automatically disqualify a candidate from running for local office. Specific restrictions vary by state and may apply to certain felony convictions, particularly those related to election fraud or public corruption. Check your state's specific statutes with the city clerk or a local election attorney.
How do I find the specific requirements to run for mayor in my city?
Start with your city clerk's website — search for "candidate filing" or "run for office." Your state's division of elections website is the second resource. Ballotpedia maintains city-by-city election information as a secondary confirmation source. Understanding how local elections work in your specific state and city is essential preparation.
What is a filing fee and how much does it cost to get on the ballot?
A filing fee is a payment made to the city clerk to have your name placed on the official election ballot. For mayoral races in mid-size American cities, filing fees typically range from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars. Some cities allow candidates to substitute a petition with the required number of registered voter signatures in lieu of the fee. The total cost to run for mayor is significantly higher than the filing fee alone.
